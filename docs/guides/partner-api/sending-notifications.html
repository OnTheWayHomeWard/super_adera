Sending notifications

POST /v1/notifications: ask Adera to reach one user with one approved template. Every field, how Adera picks the channel, idempotency with externalId, and the rules for callbackUrl.

Overview

You tell Adera who (the user's pseudonymous id for your app), what (a template id and its values) and optionally where to report (a callback URL). Adera answers immediately whether it accepted the request, and delivers in the background. The answer 202 QUEUED means "accepted and stored, we will deliver"; it does not mean delivered. The result comes by callback or by polling.

Why asynchronous

SMS gateways, email servers and Firebase are slow and sometimes down. Adera stores your request first (so it cannot be lost), then retries with backoff for as long as you allowed (ttlSeconds). Your request never waits for a telecom operator.

Endpoint

POST/v1/notificationsSend a notification to one user
Who may call
A mini-app server: valid ADERA-SIG-1 signature, key of this deployment's environment, app status allowing calls, caller on the allowed address list.
Headers
X-Adera-Key-Id, X-Adera-Timestamp, X-Adera-Signature, Content-Type: application/json
Rate limit
600 requests per 60 s per client IP (gateway) and the per-app quotas
Body limit
64 KiB (413 request_too_large); a JSON object is required
Idempotent
Yes, on externalId

Purpose

Tell a user something that matters about your service (a job was accepted, a ride arrived, a payment is due) in a way that reaches them whether or not they have your mini app open, without you holding their phone number or email address.

Reserved keys: notificationId and appId (Adera adds them) and the names Firebase reserves (from, message_type, anything starting with google or gcm): using one is 400 validation_failed with fields.data. Not used for SMS or email. Put ids, not secrets or personal data.

Unknown fields are refused (validation_failed, "unknown field"): a typo such as callbackURL does not pass silently. You never choose a channel or a language.

Example request

printf '%s' '{"externalId":"doc-cb1","userId":"'$ABEBE'","templateId":"'$JOB'",
 "params":{"name":"Abebe","reference":"J-1001"},
 "callbackUrl":"https://partner.example/adera/hook","ttlSeconds":3600,"data":{"jobId":"1001","screen":"job"}}' > body.json
./adera_call.sh POST /v1/notifications body.json

(The captured run used a callback receiver on the developer machine instead of partner.example, which the dev stack allows.) Python and Node.js versions of the same call are in Signing: call("POST", "/v1/notifications", {...}).

Use the answer: store notificationId and channel next to your own record; mark your record "notification requested", not "user informed"; wait for the callback or poll for the final status.

A rejected request (any 4xx or 503) stores nothing and uses no quota, so you can fix it and send again, even with the same externalId.

What Adera checks, in order

  1. Signature, key, environment, app status and caller address (Signing).
  2. Body shape and every field (400), all problems reported together in fields.
  3. Replay check: if this externalId exists, answer 200 replayed (same request) or 409 (different). Nothing below runs for a replay.
  4. Template: exists for your app (404), active and inside its window (422 template_*).
  5. params against the template (422 param_invalid).
  6. User: synthetic-user rule (PROD), the id resolves to an active user (422 user_unreachable).
  7. Channel choice and capability (403 capability_not_granted or 422 user_unreachable).
  8. Mute check: a muted channel is stored as SUPPRESSED and answered 200 (no quota used).
  9. Quota (429): the request is counted and stored atomically.
  10. Stored as QUEUED, 202.

Channel selection

You never name a channel. The template kind and the user's contact data decide, and your app's capabilities must allow it:

Template kindRuleCapability needed
DIRECT1. SMS if the user has a phone number and your app may send SMS. 2. Otherwise email if the user has an email address and your app may send email. 3. If the user has a phone but your app lacks notify.sms (and no usable email), or has only an email but your app lacks notify.email: 403 capability_not_granted naming the missing capability. 4. The user has neither phone nor email: 422 user_unreachable.notify.sms or notify.email
IN_APPThe user's inbox inside the Adera app, plus a push notification to each of their registered devices. Always this channel, whatever contact data the user has.notify.inapp

Observed on the dev stack with the throwaway app (all DIRECT unless noted):

UserApp capabilitiesResult
phone and emailSMS and email granted202, channel SMS
phone and emailnotify.sms DENIED202, channel EMAIL (falls back)
phone onlynotify.sms DENIED403 {"error":"capability_not_granted","capability":"notify.sms"}
email onlyemail granted202, channel EMAIL
email onlynotify.email SUSPENDED403 {"error":"capability_not_granted","capability":"notify.email"}
phone and emailnotify.email SUSPENDED, SMS granted202, channel SMS
any (IN_APP template)notify.inapp DENIED403 {"error":"capability_not_granted","capability":"notify.inapp"}
  • There is no fallback after acceptance. If the chosen channel is muted by the user, the request is SUPPRESSED, it is not re-sent by another channel. If an SMS fails permanently, it is FAILED, not re-sent by email.
  • The contact data are read again at delivery time. If the user removed their phone or email between acceptance and delivery, the notification ends as UNREACHABLE with reason no_phone or no_email.
  • Whoever needs to know which channel was used reads channel in the answer.
  • If you need a message to reach a user by every channel, send one request per template kind with different externalIds.

Idempotency with externalId

Networks fail. If your request times out you cannot know whether Adera stored it. Send it again with the same externalId and the same content: you get the stored result and nothing is sent twice.

first request                          -> 202 {"notificationId":"464e21b3-...","externalId":"doc-i1","status":"QUEUED",...}
same request again                     -> 200 {"notificationId":"464e21b3-...","externalId":"doc-i1","status":"QUEUED",...,"replayed":true}
same externalId, params changed        -> 409 {"error":"external_id_reused"}
same request, JSON keys in another order -> 200 {... "replayed":true}
  • "The same request" means the same templateId, userId, params, data, callbackUrl and ttlSeconds (key order and whitespace do not matter; an omitted ttlSeconds equals 86400). Changing any of them is 409.
  • The replay answer shows the current status (it may be SENT by now), so a replay doubles as a status read.
  • externalId is unique per app and environment: the same value in DEV and PROD are unrelated notifications.
  • A request that was refused (4xx, 429, 503) was not stored; after fixing the cause you may reuse its externalId.
  • Two identical requests arriving at the same moment: one is accepted, the other is answered as a replay.
  • Replays do not use quota. The stored record is kept: there is no expiry of externalIds today (no clean-up job exists), so choose ids that are unique over time (include your own business id, not only a counter you might reset).
Choosing the id

Make it the id of the event, not of a request attempt: job-1001-accepted, not a random UUID per HTTP call. Then retries after crashes are safe, and two workers that both react to the same event cannot notify twice.

callbackUrl rules

If you give a callback URL Adera POSTs the final status there (payload, headers, retries). The URL is chosen by the partner, so Adera protects its own network from being attacked through it (SSRF: server-side request forgery). The rules, from the code:

RuleChecked whenResult of breaking it
At most 500 characters, begins with https:// or http://when you send400 validation_failed on callbackUrl (ftp://, a missing scheme or 501+ characters were refused in testing)
Scheme must be httpswhen Adera sends the callbackCallback refused (scheme_not_https), not retried. http:// is accepted at the door and works only on deployments where Adera enabled private callbacks for local testing (the dev stack here).
No credentials in the URL (https://user:pass@host/)when sendingCallback refused (invalid_url), not retried
Port: default (443) or 8443 onlywhen sendingCallback refused (port_not_allowed)
The host name must resolve, and every address it resolves to must be a public internet addresswhen sending (each time, so DNS changes are noticed)Callback refused (host_not_resolvable or address_not_public). Refused ranges: loopback (127.0.0.0/8, ::1), private (10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16, fc00::/7), link-local and cloud metadata (169.254.0.0/16, fe80::/10), carrier-grade NAT (100.64/10), "this network" (0/8), documentation and benchmark ranges (192.0.2/24, 198.51.100/24, 203.0.113/24, 198.18/15, 192.0.0/24), multicast, and reserved 240/4.
Redirects are never followedwhen sendingA 3xx answer counts as a failure (not 2xx) and is retried; make the callback URL final
Timeoutswhen sending5 s to connect, 8 s total; slower answers count as failures and are retried
Gotcha: a bad callback URL is accepted

The refusals marked "when sending" happen later, in the background, after 202. A callback URL pointing at a private address, at port 8080, or using http:// in production is accepted by POST /v1/notifications and the callback is then silently dropped (nothing is reported to you; the callback is marked failed on Adera's side without retries). Poll the status endpoint if in doubt, and test your callback URL in DEV. The dev stack documented here relaxes the address and scheme rules (PARTNER_ALLOW_PRIVATE_CALLBACKS) so that callbacks to a developer machine work; production does not, so the refusals could not be shown live on this stack (from the code, not reproduced). See known issues.

Practical advice: use a stable https URL with a certificate from a public authority on port 443, accept POST only, verify the signature, and answer 2xx quickly (do the work after answering). You may put a query string in the URL (it is signed as part of the path), for example ?tenant=7; do not put secrets in it, it appears in your and Adera's logs.

Capabilities

A capability is a permission staff grant per app: notify.sms, notify.email, notify.inapp. States: GRANTED, DENIED, SUSPENDED. Only GRANTED counts. Without it, a request that would need the channel is refused:

HTTP 403
{"error": "capability_not_granted", "capability": "notify.sms"}

Fix: ask Adera staff to grant the capability named in capability. A change takes effect within about 15 seconds. (Other capabilities exist for features that are not available to partners yet, such as user.contact or pay.request; see data available.)
