Capabilities

What a mini app may ask for: the catalog, granting, denying and suspending, reasons, and what each capability does.

Overview

A capability is one thing a mini app is allowed to ask for: a piece of user data, a kind of location, the right to send SMS, the right to ask for a payment. By default an app has none. Staff grant them one by one, and can deny or suspend them again. The capabilities that are granted are what the user sees on the app's detail page ("this app can ..." with the reason you wrote), and, for the three notify.* capabilities, what the notification service enforces on every partner call.

Why this existsLeast privilege: a partner gets only what its product needs, each grant is a deliberate, audited decision, and any sensitive grant must come with a plain-language reason that is shown to users. Suspending is the quick brake when a partner misuses one capability but the rest of the app should keep working.

Be clear with partners

Only the notify.* capabilities change what the platform does today. user.*, location.* and pay.request are recorded, audited and shown to users, but no service checks them yet because the in-app bridge and the payment feature are not built. Grant them anyway when the product needs them, so that the consent text users see is already right when the bridge ships.

States and reasons

StateMeaningEffect today
GRANTEDThe app may use it.Counts for the notification checks; listed to users in the catalog with its description and reason.
DENIEDReviewed and refused (or never approved).Not usable; not shown to users. The reason you write is kept for staff.
SUSPENDEDWas granted, switched off for now (misuse, investigation).Not usable; not shown to users. Switch back with GRANTED.
  • A capability that has never been set is simply not granted (default deny). There is no "delete": set DENIED.
  • DENIED and SUSPENDED behave the same for the platform; the difference is the story you tell staff and the audit trail.
  • Reasons are objects like {"en": "To show technicians near you.", "am": "..."} (en, am, om). When you GRANT a capability whose reasonRequired is true (user.full_name, user.contact, user.address, location.coarse, location.fine, pay.request) an English reason of at least 10 characters is required; Amharic and Oromo are optional but recommended. Write it for the user ("To show technicians near you"), not for engineers: it is shown verbatim.
  • Reasons are optional for the other capabilities and for DENIED or SUSPENDED (stored, not shown to users).

Side effect: default notification limits

Granting notify.sms, notify.email or notify.inapp also creates that channel's notification limits with conservative defaults, but only if the app has no limits for that channel yet (existing limits are never touched, and suspending or denying never removes them). An admin raises them per app later (see Notification limits).

Purpose. Sets the state of one capability for one app, and (when granting) the reason users will read. The call replaces that capability's state and reason; it is an upsert, not a patch. When to use it. To approve what a partner asked for, to refuse it, or to suspend a capability during a misuse investigation.

Effects, audit, gotchas

  • Audit: capability.set, meta {"capability": "notify.sms", "state": "SUSPENDED"} (the reason text is not in the audit event; it is stored on the capability).
  • Takes effect in the notification service within about 15 seconds. After you suspend notify.sms, new DIRECT sends are not made by SMS (see the next section).
  • Messages already accepted keep their channel; they are not re-routed when a capability changes later.
  • To remove a grant, set DENIED (or SUSPENDED if it should come back).
  • The reason of a GRANTED capability is shown to users verbatim: re-read it before saving. Changing it later is another PUT with the same state and a new reason.

Scenario

A home-services partner asks for city-level location. You grant location.coarse with "To show technicians near you" and the Amharic text. Users who open the app's details now see that line. Later you find the partner sends too many SMS: you suspend notify.sms only, and tell them to use in-app messages until you re-grant it.

How the notify.* capabilities act on partner sends

The partner never chooses a channel. The template kind and the user's contact details decide, and the capabilities gate each channel:

Template kindChannel chosenNeeds
IN_APPIN_APP (inbox entry plus push to the user's devices)notify.inapp GRANTED, else 403 capability_not_granted (with capability: notify.inapp).
DIRECTSMS if the user has a phone and notify.sms is granted; otherwise EMAIL if the user has an email and notify.email is grantedAt least one of the two matching capabilities. If the user's reachable contact needs a capability that is not granted: 403 capability_not_granted; if no contact fits: 422 user_unreachable.

Real partner-side results from the test (the DIRECT template to a test user who has both a phone and an email):

notify.sms SUSPENDED, notify.email GRANTED -> falls back to email:
HTTP 202  channel: EMAIL

both SUSPENDED:
HTTP 403  {"error": "capability_not_granted", "capability": "notify.sms"}

both GRANTED again -> SMS:
HTTP 202  channel: SMS

So suspending SMS alone can quietly move a partner's traffic to email; suspend both channels if you want the DIRECT sends to stop.

Not built yet

  • Enforcement of user.*, location.* and pay.request, and the per-user consent step (they arrive with the in-app bridge).
  • A history view of a capability's old reasons (only the audit log shows that it changed).
  • Adding new capability keys without a code change.
