Known issues and not built yet

What is missing and the decisions taken where the design was silent.

Not built yet

  • The bridge. Specified in this guide; the page side (window.adera, the dialog, the settings sheet, getLocation) is for the super app team. Until it exists, a mini app's page cannot trigger the dialog; everything behind it works through the user API.
  • Consent expiry. Consent does not expire (the default of the design). A review-after-N-months policy is not built.
  • Payments. pay.request can be granted and answered like the others, but payments are not built; the design says a payment confirmation is asked every time rather than stored.
  • A street address. The platform has no postal address: user.address releases the saved place (country, region, city). Coordinates are never released.
  • Staff browsing of who answered what. By design only counts.

Decisions where the design was silent

  • A DRAFT or unknown app is 404 not_found; a PAUSED or KILLED app is 403 app_not_live; Disconnect works on any non-draft app.
  • user.basic is reported GRANTED with no row until the user disconnects; the menu cannot switch it off alone (409 use_disconnect); launch reconnects it.
  • The profile call returns whatever is allowed; ?fields= says what the partner needs, and a missing one is 403 consent_required. Without it, a user who only opened the app gives a normal 200 with granted: ["user.basic"].
  • A notification is refused for NOT_ASKED too (default deny), and there is no fallback to another channel the user allowed.
  • Callbacks are sent only to apps with a callback address; one user.disconnected per disconnect, not one user.consent_changed per permission; at least once, possibly late.
  • The launch token is signed by the service that holds the Adera signing key; the user service decides who gets one and with which scopes.

Known issues

  • The launch response lists consents in the registry's alphabetical order (not "user.basic first").
  • Notification callbacks and consent callbacks share one queue; a partner address that keeps failing delays neither type beyond its own retries, but both stop after 24 hours.
