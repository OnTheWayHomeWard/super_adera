Bridge Lab: testing the mini-app bridge by hand

Bridge Lab is a test mini app. You open it inside the real Adera app and walk through a checklist of manual, step-by-step tests of every way the super app and a mini app interact, with full logs of the communication and of the mini app's backend.

Audience

Super-app developers who build the mini-app bridge, and the owner who checks what works. It is a test tool, not a product, and not an automatic test runner: a person taps Allow or Deny, opens the menu, pauses the app in the backoffice. Nothing here links outside this folder.

Overview

The super app will host mini apps in a WebView and give them a JavaScript bridge (window.adera) to ask for permissions and the location. Around the bridge there is a launch token in the URL, a permission menu, Disconnect, callbacks to the mini app's server, partner calls for the profile and notifications, and deep links from banners and notifications. Bridge Lab has one test for each of these behaviours, 104 tests in 12 groups, and for each one it shows:

  • the goal and what must be true before you start;
  • numbered steps: either Run step (Bridge Lab calls the bridge or its own backend and shows expected against actual with a verdict) or an instruction for a person;
  • when something is missing, a plain sentence saying what the super app must do, taken from the bridge specification;
  • a log of every bridge message in both directions and every backend event (launch-token checks, signed partner calls with their trace ids, callbacks received).
Why a mini app and not a unit test

The bridge lives in the Flutter app and its dialogs need a person. The only honest test is a real mini app running inside the real super app, talking to the real backend. Bridge Lab is that mini app, registered like any partner, with every capability granted, a callback address and a partner key.

Conventions

  • Super app = the Adera app (Flutter). Mini app = a partner web app shown inside it. Bridge = window.adera.
  • Bridge Lab runs at http://localhost:8095 on the developer laptop (a second allowed origin on port 8096). The phone reaches it with adb reverse.
  • Its backend calls the gateway as a partner would: ADERA-SIG-1 signatures with Bridge Lab's own private key.
  • Results are kept on the phone (the page's local storage) until you reset them; the report can be uploaded to the Bridge Lab server and downloaded on the laptop.

Glossary

TermMeaning
Step verdictWhat Bridge Lab concluded for one step: pass, fail, not developed, info, skipped.
Test statusWhat the person decided for the whole test: Not run, Pass, Fail, Not developed yet, Skipped. Bridge Lab suggests one from the steps.
Not developedThe super app does not do it yet: no window.adera, a missing method, or no answer at all within the time limit.
FailThe super app did something, but not what the specification says (wrong state, wrong id, no "adera": 1, an unknown error code...).
Not specifiedThe documents imply a behaviour but give no contract. The test records what happens and proposes what the super app should do.
Launch tokenThe short-lived signed JWT the super app puts in the URL (?launch=) when it opens a mini app.
Raw messageA message Bridge Lab posts straight to the AderaBridge channel, bypassing window.adera, to test how bad messages are answered.
