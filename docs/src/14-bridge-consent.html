14. Mini app bridge and user consent

How a mini app gets data about a user and from the device, how the user decides what it may have, and how we stop it from taking more. Permissions are asked at the moment of need, one at a time, like Android, not all at launch. Design agreed on 2026-11-04 and built in the order of section 10 (steps 1 to 6 on 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05); section 13 is the bridge specification for the super app team. Every request and response in sections 4 to 6 and 12 was captured from the running stack; the messages of the bridge itself (section 13) are protocol examples because the bridge is the super app team's to build.

Status

Backend built (steps 1 to 6); the bridge itself is specified in section 13 and not built (it lives in the super app). Built so far: step 1 (purpose per capability), step 2 (the consent endpoints in user-service), step 3 (counts per capability in the backoffice), step 4 (notify gate, mute as consent), step 5 (launch token, partner profile), step 6 (partner callbacks) and step 7 (this specification and the shareable guide docs/guides/app-permissions/). The whole scenario of section 7 is run end to end by tests/e2e/permissions.py. Decisions taken are in sections 11 and 12.

1. The short answer

  • The manifest is our registry, not a file the mini app hosts. A mini app must not describe its own powers. The capabilities an app may ever ask for are the ones staff granted in the backoffice (user.basic, location.fine, ...). We add a purpose sentence per capability, written by the partner and approved by staff, so the user is told why. The mini app cannot change that text.
  • Ask at the moment of need, one permission at a time. Opening a mini app shows no dialog. When the mini app first needs location, it asks for location, the super app shows our approved text, and the user answers. A granted answer is remembered; a denied one is remembered too and limits how often the app may ask again (section 5).
  • The backend decides, always. The super app's bridge (the JavaScript the mini app calls) only drives the dialog. A mini app is third-party code inside a WebView, so the bridge cannot be the gate. Every data request ends in a backend check that ignores what the bridge said.
  • Three gates, in this order: (1) did Adera grant this capability to this app, and is the app LIVE? (2) did this user say yes to this capability for this app, and is it still yes? (3) is the request for exactly what that capability covers? Fail any gate and nothing is returned.
  • The mini app decides what a "no" means. It can show "we cannot continue without your location", the reason, and an Allow button that asks again, or open the permission settings once asking is blocked. The server does not know which permissions an app cannot live without.
  • The menu sheet always works. Every mini app's top menu lists its permissions with a switch each; that is the only way to undo a block (section 5).
  • Location is different. We never hold the live position. It is read from the phone, so it needs the phone's own permission as well as ours. The backend records consent and the super app hands the reading to the mini app (section 3).

2. The three gates

GateQuestionWho knowsFails with
1. GrantedIs the capability granted to the app, and is the app LIVE (not DRAFT, PAUSED or KILLED)?registry-service (exists today)403 capability_not_granted or 403 app_not_live
2. ConsentedDid this user allow it, and not revoke it since? Is the user ACTIVE?user-service (new table)403 consent_required (body says the state: NOT_ASKED, DENIED, BLOCKED)
3. In scopeIs the thing asked for covered by the capability? (location.coarse gives a city, never coordinates; user.contact never gives the address.)The data endpoint itself403 outside_capability

Gate 1 is checked on every call, so pausing or killing an app, or removing a capability in the backoffice, takes effect immediately. The 15-second revoked-key cache we already accepted is the only delay.

3. Two kinds of data, two paths

KindExamplesHeld byHow the mini app gets it
Server-helduser.basic (the pseudonymous id), user.full_name, user.contact, user.addressuser-servicePath A or B below
Device-heldlocation.coarse, location.fineThe phoneThe bridge asks the phone; the backend only checks and records consent
Action, no datanotify.sms, notify.email, notify.inapp, pay.requestnotification-service, payments laterThe mini app backend calls the partner API; the gate is applied there

Path A: through the bridge (the mini app's frontend asks)

The mini app calls adera.getProfile(). The super app calls the backend with the user's session, the backend runs the three gates and returns the data, and the super app passes it into the page. The data lands in the mini app's browser code. Fine for low-risk fields (a first name to say "Hi Hana"). Quick to build, but anything in the page can be copied by the partner's scripts or by a bad ad or library the partner loaded.

Path B: through the mini app's backend (recommended for identifying data)

The super app opens the mini app with a short-lived launch token (section 4). The mini app's backend verifies the token and then calls Adera with a signed request (the signing scheme partners already use) to read what the user consented to. Data never travels through the browser, it is logged on our side per call, and the partner's keys, rate limits and environment checks all apply.

Recommendation: support both behind the same three gates. Use Path B for user.full_name, user.contact and user.address. Allow Path A only for user.basic and for device data.

Location

The phone asks the user for its own location permission the first time (the system dialog). Our consent is a separate yes. The bridge only gives a reading if both are yes, and otherwise returns denied or blocked without telling the mini app which of the two said no. If the mini app's backend needs the position, the mini app frontend sends it; we do not store positions on our servers.

4. Launching a mini app: the launch token built 2026-11-04

Opening a mini app creates a short session. The super app asks the backend for a launch token and loads the mini app URL (the one registered in the backoffice) with the token. The token is a signed JWT using the same Adera signing key as callbacks and responses; partners already fetch the public key at /.well-known/jwks.json. The private key stays in notification-service: user-service decides who gets a token and with which scopes, then asks notification-service (POST /internal/launch-tokens, internal secret) to sign it.

Claims: iss adera, aud the app's id, sub the pseudonymous id u_p_... (never the real user id; it is the id to use in /v1/users/{sub}/profile), env dev or prod, scopes the capabilities the user allowed at this moment (here the staff-granted ones the user allowed: the pseudonymous id and the two notification channels), exp five minutes ahead (exp - iat = 300), jti a random id. The mini app's backend verifies it with the public key (checked for this very token with openssl dgst -sha256 -verify against the key built from the JWKS: "Verified OK"; runnable Python and Node examples verify_launch_token.py and verify-launch-token.js are in the partner guide, tested against this stack: a changed signature gives InvalidSignature / "bad signature", a token for another app is refused with "this token is for another app").

5. Permission states and what we store

Each (user, app, capability) is in one of four states:

StateMeaningA request from the mini app
NOT_ASKEDNever asked (also: no row)Shows the dialog
GRANTEDThe user said yesReturns granted with no dialog
DENIEDThe user said no onceShows the dialog one more time (the mini app should explain why first)
BLOCKEDThe user said no twice in a rowReturns blocked with no dialog. Only the menu switch can change it

Rules: answering yes in the dialog sets GRANTED and the denial count to 0. The first no sets DENIED (count 1); the second no sets BLOCKED (count 2). A switch in the menu always works: switching on sets GRANTED, count 0; switching off sets DENIED, count 0, so the mini app may ask again later. A capability not granted to the app by staff never gets a dialog: the request returns not_available at once. There is no timer that unblocks; only the menu does.

The server enforces this, not the bridge: a dialog is only allowed after the server has issued a one-time prompt for it (section 6), and an answer without a prompt is refused unless it comes from the menu.

New tables in user-service (it owns users and the per-app pseudonymous ids):

TableColumnsNotes
app_consentuser id, app id, capability, state, denial count, decided at, source (dialog or menu), prompt id and prompt expiry (while a dialog is open)One current row per user, app and capability. No row means NOT_ASKED.
consent_eventuser id, app id, capability, from state, to state, at, sourceAppend only. Proof that the user agreed and when.

The menu sheet lists only capabilities that are currently granted to the app (gate 1). If staff remove a capability its row stays but is inert and no longer listed. If staff grant a new one, nothing happens until the mini app asks for it.

Built (step 1): the purpose sentence, as it works today

The text was already stored next to the grant under the name reason; it is now called purpose everywhere (database column renamed by migration V7__capability_purpose.sql; the old request field name reason is still accepted as an alias). It is one sentence per language (en, am, om), at most 160 characters, one line, no link, and required in English (10 characters or more) for the capabilities whose catalog entry says purposeRequired: true. Decision: a grant without a purpose is allowed for the other capabilities (user.basic, notify.*); the dialog then shows the catalog's default sentence (defaultPurpose), so no dialog is ever empty. Each capability also has a catalog label (the dialog headline), returned by GET /admin/api/v1/capabilities and in the public catalog permissions. Other services read label, purpose and default from registry-service in the permissions field of GET /internal/apps/{appId}/context (only GRANTED capabilities are listed there).

6. Endpoints

All user endpoints use the existing gateway route for signed-in users. They live under /me/apps/{app}, so they do not collide with the public catalog's /apps/{app}/permissions (what an app is allowed to use, visible to anyone). {app} is the id or slug.

EndpointPurposeCalled by
GET /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/permissions builtEvery capability granted to the app with label, purpose and this user's state. Menu sheet and getPermissionSuper app
POST /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/permissions/{capability}/request builtThe mini app asked. Returns the state and, only if a dialog is allowed, a one-time prompt with the approved textSuper app, from requestPermission
PUT /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/permissions/{capability}/answer builtThe dialog answer, with the prompt id from the previous callSuper app
PUT /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/permissions/{capability} builtThe menu switch: sets GRANTED or DENIED, resets the count, unblocksSuper app, menu sheet
DELETE /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/permissions builtDisconnect: everything to DENIED, count 0, and a user.disconnected callback to the partnerSuper app
GET /api/v1/me/permissions builtSettings screen: every app that holds at least one permission, with what it can seeSuper app
POST /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/launch builtLaunch token (section 4)Super app
GET /v1/users/{alias}/profile (partner API) builtOnly the fields the user allowed, nothing elseMini app backend, signed
GET /admin/api/v1/apps/{id}/permissions builtBackoffice: per capability, counts of granted, denied and blocked users (counts only, no list)Staff

The bridge, for mini app developers

Four calls: adera.requestPermission(capability), adera.getPermission(capability), adera.openPermissionSettings() and adera.getLocation(). The full specification (message format, origin rule, rules, error codes) is section 13.

7. Scenario: Demo Ride, start to finish

  2. Hana taps Demo Ride. No dialog appears. The super app calls POST /launch and opens the mini app with the launch token.
  3. Hana taps "Find a ride". The mini app calls adera.requestPermission("location.fine"). The super app shows "Demo Ride would like your precise location. Find a driver near you." Hana taps Allow. The phone's own location dialog follows and she allows it. The mini app gets granted and getLocation() returns a position.
  4. The mini app backend verifies the token and calls GET /profile. Name was never requested, so it returns only the alias and "granted": ["user.basic"]. (user.basic is the pseudonymous id and needs no dialog on its own; see section 11.)
  5. At the end of the trip the mini app wants to text the driver's code. It calls requestPermission("notify.sms"). Hana taps Deny. The mini app gets denied, shows "We can send the code in the app instead", and uses notify.inapp (asking for that permission the same way if needed). Had it sent the SMS anyway, the partner API would have answered 403 consent_required and no SMS would leave.
  6. The mini app later asks for notify.sms again, with an explanation screen first. Hana denies again. The state is now BLOCKED.
  7. Next day the mini app tries once more and gets blocked without any dialog. It shows "Text messages are switched off" and a button that calls adera.openPermissionSettings(). Hana turns SMS on in the sheet, and the next SMS goes through.
  8. Hana opens the top menu and turns location off. The next getLocation() returns denied, and the mini app shows "We cannot continue without your location" and an Allow button that asks again.
  9. Hana taps "Disconnect". Everything becomes DENIED, the mini app receives a user.disconnected callback (section 8), and the next /profile returns 403.

8. Notifications, revocation and callbacks

  • Consent for notify.* joins the existing mute preferences. Today a user can mute an app's notifications (stored in user-service) and the app's own notify limits come from the registry. I suggest one idea: the notify consent is the mute switch. Denied means muted. That removes a second place where the user would have to say the same thing. Existing mutes would be converted to DENIED on migration.
  • Revoke is immediate. The gate reads the consent row on each call. There is no cached "allowed".
  • Tell the partner. When consent changes, we send a signed callback (the retry-for-24-hours mechanism already exists): user.consent_changed with the alias, the capability and the new state, and user.disconnected on disconnect. A partner that kept the user's name is expected to delete it on user.disconnected. We can require this in the partner terms, but we cannot enforce it technically once the data has left.
  • Suspended or deleted users fail gate 2 for everything.

11. Decisions taken (2026-11-04)

  1. Ask at the moment of need, never all at launch. One permission per request; there is no batch request.
  2. Two denials block. After the second no the dialog is not shown again; the mini app can only point the user to the permission settings.
  3. Only the menu unblocks. No timer. The menu switch also resets the denial count.
  4. No required flag. The mini app decides what to do when a permission is refused.
  5. Per-capability consent (not one yes per app).
  6. Notification consent replaces mute (mute becomes DENIED on migration).
  7. Identifying data goes through the mini app's backend with a signed request; the browser path is only for user.basic and device data.

Still open, with my default: does consent expire? (default: no.) Does pay.request need a confirmation on every payment rather than a stored yes? (default: yes; designed when payments are built.) Does user.basic, the pseudonymous id, need a dialog? (default: no, the id is not personal data and the mini app needs it to identify the user at all; it is granted without asking.)

12. Decisions taken while building (where this page was silent)

The wording and numbers above won everywhere the code allowed it. Where the page said nothing, this is what was chosen (each is easy to change):

  • Step 1. The existing per-capability reason already was the purpose text (per language, required in English for sensitive capabilities, shown to users), so it was renamed purpose instead of adding a second field; reason stays accepted as an input alias. New rules: at most 160 characters, one line, no link. A grant without a purpose is allowed where the catalog does not require one, and the dialog then shows the catalog's default sentence.
  • Step 2, app state. A DRAFT or unknown app answers 404 not_found (users never see drafts); a PAUSED or KILLED app answers 403 app_not_live (the page's gate 1 wording). Disconnect is allowed for any existing non-draft app, also paused, so a user can always leave.
  • Step 2, errors. 409 prompt_invalid (no ticket, wrong ticket, used, or tied to another user, app or capability), 409 prompt_expired, 403 capability_not_granted for answer and menu on a capability staff did not grant, 409 use_disconnect for switching user.basic off, 403 account_suspended / account_deleted for a user who is not ACTIVE. All use the {"error": code} shape; validation uses validation_failed with fields.
  • Step 2, user.basic. Reported GRANTED without a row while granted to the app (open decision default). Disconnect stores DENIED for it too (so the next profile call can be refused); it is switched on again by the menu or, from step 5, by opening the app. It cannot be refused in a dialog or switched off alone.
  • Step 2, shapes. The list endpoint returns a JSON array. Each item also carries denialCount, canAskAgain (a dialog would be shown on the next request) and decidedAt. The settings overview lists an app only while the user has at least one GRANTED permission there; only permissions that are still granted by staff are shown. The answer to Disconnect is {"disconnected": true, "changed": [...]}.
  • Step 2, ticket. pr_ plus 24 random hex characters, valid 5 minutes, single use, replaced by the next request. Never-asked permissions stay NOT_ASKED after Disconnect; a BLOCKED one becomes DENIED with count 0 (the page says "everything to DENIED, count 0").
  • Step 2, rate limit. 30 per minute per address for the dialog request route, matched in the gateway by a regular expression on the path (APISIX does not match a wildcard in the middle of a path); everything else under /api/v1/me keeps 300 per minute.
  • Step 3, who serves the counts. user-service, because it owns the consent data; it asks registry-service (existing internal call) which capabilities the app has. The gateway sends only this one path to user-service (route admin-app-permissions, matched by regular expression). Any staff role may read it (counts are not personal data). user.basic counts users with a pseudonymous id for the app: granted = not disconnected, denied = disconnected. Deleted users are not counted, suspended ones are; capabilities staff removed are not listed. The backoffice shows the numbers in a Users column of the Capabilities tab.
  • Step 4, the gate. A partner request is refused with 403 consent_required (body: capability, state) when the user's answer for the notify capability of the channel Adera picked is not GRANTED; NOT_ASKED is refused too (the page: DENIED, BLOCKED and NOT_ASKED all refuse). The capability gate (403 capability_not_granted) still comes first. "Recorded the way other refusals are": like the other 4xx refusals it is the HTTP answer to the partner and leaves no notification row and no quota use. The channel is still chosen as before (SMS if the user has a phone and the app has notify.sms, else email, in-app for in-app templates) and there is no fallback to another channel the user allowed, keeping the old mute behaviour.
  • Step 4, delivery time. Consent is re-read right before every send: a user who switched the channel off after the request was accepted ends SUPPRESSED with reason recipient_opted_out (the only way SUPPRESSED by user choice still happens).
  • Step 4, mute endpoints. GET/PUT /api/v1/me/apps/{appId}/preferences keep their shape and gain states. muteX is true for DENIED and BLOCKED; muteX: true sets DENIED (count 0) and false sets GRANTED (the menu switch, source MENU), also for a channel staff did not grant (the answer stays inert). A user who never answered shows "not muted" but is refused until they allow it: default-deny. This is a breaking change for old clients that assumed muted = false means messages flow; documented, no real users.
  • Step 4, synthetic users. u_p_test_ok, _unreachable and _muted behave as before. Three new ones, u_p_test_noconsent, _denied and _blocked, show the consent gate in DEV.
  • Step 4, seed data. A seeded user allows every channel of the apps they use except the ones they muted (seed_scenarios.py, step notifyconsent); the e2e scripts grant consent through the same endpoints before sending.
  • Step 5, who signs. The launch token is signed by notification-service (it holds the one Adera signing key, loaded from Infisical) through an internal endpoint; user-service never sees the private key. The partner signature check for /v1/** lives in notification-service too, so the profile endpoint is served there and reads the user's data from user-service by an internal call (user data stays owned by user-service, which releases only what is GRANTED).
  • Step 5, the profile. ?fields= (optional) is the partner saying what it needs: a missing one is 403 consent_required naming it (the page's sample). Without it, whatever is allowed is returned. user.basic not granted (disconnected) is a 403 for the whole profile. A capability the app was never granted is 403 capability_not_granted (checked first). Unknown, foreign, deleted and suspended aliases are all 422 user_unreachable (like sends). user.address returns {countryCode, region, city} from the profile's saved place: the platform has no street address, and coordinates are never released. user.contact returns phone and email separately, each only if the user has it. Synthetic users answer in DEV (u_p_test_ok with fake data; the consent-state ones as for notifications) and are refused in PROD.
  • Step 5, launch details. iss is the string adera; env is the deployment's (dev/prod); consents in the response lists every capability staff granted with the user's state in the registry's alphabetical order (not the page's illustrative order); url is null when no entry URL is registered; the call reconnects a disconnected user.basic (event source LAUNCH) and nothing else. Rate limit 60 per minute per address (route me-launch).
  • Step 6, where the callback URL lives. The page says "only to apps with a callback URL configured", but the platform only knew a callback URL per notification request. A new optional callbackUrl per app and environment (registry app_environment.callback_url, V8) is set by staff next to the allowed server addresses (same endpoint, own field, backoffice Environments tab); DEV and PROD can differ. The notification-service reads the one of its own environment from the registry context (cached 15 s). No URL, no callback.
  • Step 6, what is sent. user.consent_changed for every real change (dialog answer, menu switch, mute switch, user.basic reconnecting on launch), carrying alias, capability, state, at, environment; not for an unchanged state or a changed refusal count. user.disconnected once per disconnect (not one consent_changed per permission) with capabilities taken back. Only users the app knows (it has an alias from a launch) are reported. No real user id anywhere. The migration of old mutes sends nothing.
  • Step 6, reliability. At least once and unordered after failures: the event is written in the same transaction as the change (outbox), handed over by a worker with retries and dropped after 24 hours, then delivered by the existing callback worker (signed, 24-hour retry). The partner de-duplicates on Idempotency-Key and orders by at.
  • Step 7, the specification. Where the page named only the four methods, the specification fixes: the message envelope ({"adera": 1, "id", "method", "params"} answered by ok with result or error), bridge values as the lower-case API states (not_asked only from getPermission), openPermissionSettings resolving with the states at the moment the sheet closes, getLocation({precision}) with a coarse result of only country, region and city, a dismissed dialog rejecting with dialog_dismissed (nothing answered, the prompt expires), one dialog at a time (busy), a 30-per-minute call limit, and the error codes. The transport (JavaScript channel AderaBridge in a WebView, postMessage in a web host) is a proposal for the super app team to accept or change.

13. The bridge specification (for the super app team)

What the bridge is

The bridge is a thin layer in the super app (the Adera app, a Flutter WebView host) that lets the mini app's page ask for permissions and a location reading. It adds no authority of its own: a mini app is third-party code, so the backend decides everything (the three gates), and the bridge only drives the dialog and carries answers. Every bridge method ends in one of the user endpoints (section 6); a mini app that bypasses the bridge and calls them directly has no session of the user, and a mini app's backend can never trigger a dialog. This is a specification for the super app team: nothing in the Adera backend implements the page side.

The four methods

The super app injects window.adera into the mini app's page (only for the registered origin, see the origin rule). Every method returns a Promise.

CallResolves withWhat the super app does
adera.requestPermission(capability)"granted", "denied", "blocked" or "not_available"One permission per call; there is no batch call. Calls POST /api/v1/me/apps/{app}/permissions/{capability}/request. If the answer has a prompt, shows the dialog with prompt.label and prompt.purpose verbatim and Allow / Deny, then calls PUT .../answer with the promptId and resolves with the new state. If the state is GRANTED, BLOCKED or NOT_AVAILABLE there is no dialog and it resolves at once. A dialog dismissed without Allow or Deny (back button) answers nothing: the Promise rejects with dialog_dismissed and the prompt expires by itself.
adera.getPermission(capability)The same four values plus "not_asked"Never shows a dialog. Calls GET .../permissions and picks the capability (a capability that is not in the list resolves "not_available"). Use it to decide whether to explain first or to show a different screen.
adera.openPermissionSettings(){"permissions": {"location.fine": "granted", ...}} when the user closes the sheetOpens the app's permission sheet from the super app menu (one switch per permission, GET .../permissions, switches call PUT .../permissions/{capability}, the only way out of blocked) and resolves with the states at the moment the sheet closes.
adera.getLocation(options?)Success: {"state": "granted", "location": {"latitude": 9.0054, "longitude": 38.7636, "accuracyMeters": 12, "at": "2026-11-04T09:12:44Z"}}. Not allowed: {"state": "denied"}, "blocked", "not_asked" or "not_available"Never shows a dialog by itself. With {"precision": "fine"} (default) it needs the permission location.fine; with {"precision": "coarse"} it needs location.coarse and returns only {"place": {"countryCode": "ET", "region": "Addis Ababa", "city": "Addis Ababa"}}, never coordinates. It returns a reading only if both our consent and the phone's own location permission are yes, and otherwise says denied or blocked without telling which of the two refused (so the mini app cannot probe the phone). The reading comes from the phone; Adera never stores a position. The phone's own dialog is the system's and comes after ours (the first time).

State names on the bridge are the lower-case form of the API's states: NOT_ASKED becomes not_asked, and so on; NOT_AVAILABLE (staff did not grant the capability to the app) becomes not_available.

Message format

All messages are JSON objects with the marker "adera": 1 (the protocol version). A request has a unique id chosen by the mini app (a string of at most 64 characters), a method and params. The answer repeats the id and has ok true with a result, or ok false with an error object (code, message). A message that is not an object, has no "adera": 1, has an unknown method or a params over 1 KB is answered with an error and nothing else happens.

// mini app -> super app
{"adera": 1, "id": "m_17", "method": "requestPermission", "params": {"capability": "location.fine"}}

// super app -> mini app, success
{"adera": 1, "id": "m_17", "ok": true, "result": {"state": "granted"}}

// super app -> mini app, failure
{"adera": 1, "id": "m_17", "ok": false, "error": {"code": "busy", "message": "A permission dialog is already open."}}

Transport. In a native WebView (the Flutter app) the page posts the JSON string to a JavaScript channel named AderaBridge and the super app answers by calling window.adera._receive(json); window.adera wraps this in the four Promise methods so mini app code never touches the channel. In a web-hosted super app the page uses window.parent.postMessage(message, superAppOrigin) and the super app answers with event.source.postMessage(answer, miniAppOrigin), never with "*".

The origin rule

  • The bridge is injected only for pages whose origin (scheme, host and port) is the origin of the mini app's registered URL (entryUrl) or one of its allowedOrigins, both set by staff in the backoffice. Navigating to any other origin removes the bridge before the page loads; a page of another origin never sees window.adera.
  • Every message is checked again: in a WebView the host compares the origin of the frame that sent it, and a message from a frame whose origin is not in the list (for example an embedded advertisement) is ignored with no answer. Answers go only to the registered origin.
  • The super app sends the user's session to the backend itself. The mini app never receives the session token, the user's id or the launch scopes through the bridge.

Rules the super app must keep

  • One dialog at a time. A second requestPermission while a dialog is open is rejected with busy; there is no queue and no batch.
  • The text is ours. The dialog shows only label and purpose from the server, as plain text. The mini app cannot change it, add to it or style it.
  • The server decides. Never answer from a cached state: requestPermission always calls the server first (the state may have changed in the menu or been blocked), and getLocation checks the consent on the server before it reads the phone.
  • Show no dialog at launch. A mini app opening is POST .../launch only.
  • Limit calls: at most 30 requestPermission per minute per mini app (the gateway also limits that endpoint to 30 per minute per address). Beyond it the bridge rejects with too_many_requests.

Status

This specification is written, but the bridge is not built (the super app is a separate project). Everything behind it exists and is captured against the running stack: the endpoints it calls, the launch token and the backend profile call. The samples in this specification (the messages above) are protocol examples, not captures.
